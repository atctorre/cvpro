How to write an administrative assistant resume that proves you run the office

The administrative assistant role has a trap: almost everyone thinks they can do it, so job posts fill with generic resumes. The manager hiring is not looking for someone who "provides support". They want the person who makes the office run without them having to think about it. Your resume must prove organization, judgment and discretion with concrete examples, not adjectives.

What managers look for in an administrative assistant

  • Real tool proficiency. Excel beyond basic sums (pivot tables, VLOOKUP, filters), Word with professional formatting, PowerPoint, Outlook or Google Workspace, and any ERP, CRM or expense system you used. Name them and say what you did with them.
  • Calendar and logistics management. Coordinating multiple executives' calendars, travel, rooms, vendors. Give volume: "managed calendars for three directors with 20+ weekly meetings".
  • Document control. Physical and digital filing, correspondence, reports, meeting minutes. If you built or improved a filing system, say so.
  • Discretion. You handle payroll data, contracts, client information. Show you treat confidentiality as part of the job, not an afterthought.
  • Expense and purchasing responsibility. Petty cash, expense reports, vendor invoices reconciled without discrepancies — a trust signal that carries weight.

Mistakes that get an administrative assistant resume rejected

  • The generic summary. "Detail-oriented, organized, proactive professional" is on every resume in the stack. Replace it with a result: "Reorganized five years of records in two weeks, cutting document retrieval time".
  • Listing "Microsoft Office" with no detail. Everyone lists Office. Say what you build in Excel and what documents you produce in Word. Real skill shows in the interview test.
  • Tasks instead of ownership. "Answered phones, filed, scheduled" describes the job, not you. Write about what depended on you.
  • Formatting or spelling errors. Your resume is the first work sample. A misaligned or misspelled document disqualifies an admin before the interview.
  • Not mentioning confidential information handling. If you had access to payroll, contracts or client data, use the word "confidential". It is a selection criterion.

If you have no formal administrative experience

Many administrative functions are performed without the title: front desk at a family business, coordinating school or community events, treasurer for a club, customer-facing roles with paperwork. Rewrite them in office language: "calendar management", "expense tracking", "report preparation". Invest in proving Excel: a short certification outweighs "proficient in Office". And format your own resume as if it were your first assignment on the job — because it is.

Frequently asked questions

Should I list typing speed?

Yes, if it is strong and you can sustain it in a test. Many employers assess it during the process.

How do I prove advanced Excel?

With examples of what you produced: "Monthly expense reports with pivot tables for three departments". Add a certification if you have one. Avoid self-ratings with stars or percentages.

Should I include a photo?

No. US resumes do not include photos, and many companies set aside resumes that have them.

How long should it be?

One page. If you are an administrative assistant, your resume should prove you know how to be concise.

Is basic Spanish or another language worth listing?

Yes, with the honest level. For roles supporting bilingual teams or clients it can be decisive even when the posting does not require it.
