How to write an architect resume when the portfolio carries the weight

In architecture the portfolio decides, but the resume determines whether it gets opened. The typical mistake is treating the resume as an experimental design piece and producing something illegible — or the opposite, something so flat it contradicts the profession. What the firm principal wants is clarity, which project phase you work in, and whether you can carry a project from concept to construction detail.

What the firm principal looks for

  • Project phase. Schematic design, design development, construction documents, construction administration, permitting. Firms hire for the phase they need covered.
  • Project typology. Residential, commercial, institutional, hospitality, healthcare, interiors, urban design. With square footage and whether it was built.
  • Production software. Revit, AutoCAD, ArchiCAD, SketchUp, Rhino, Enscape, Adobe Suite. Specify which you produce construction documents in, not just images.
  • Technical documentation. CD sets, details, specifications, code analysis, consultant coordination, RFIs and submittals. This is where it shows whether you can build or only draw.
  • Licensure path. AXP hours logged, ARE divisions passed, licensed architect with state. It is the career marker firms track.

Mistakes that get an architect resume rejected

  • An experimental, illegible resume. Trying to prove design through the CV usually backfires. Clear hierarchy IS design; the rest is noise.
  • A renders-only portfolio. Without plans, sections and details, the principal assumes you cannot document. Include technical work.
  • Not stating project phase. A competition architect and a CD architect have different skills. Always specify.
  • Projects without size or status. Say whether it was built. One executed project outweighs three unbuilt competitions.
  • Omitting AXP or ARE progress. It is the standard signal of career trajectory in US practice. Its absence raises questions.

If you are a new-graduate architect

The portfolio rules, but it must show technical capability, not just visuals: include plans, sections and at least one construction detail from your thesis. If you interned, detail the project phase you worked in and what documentation you produced. Learn Revit at production level — the market moved to BIM and graduates who only know AutoCAD and SketchUp compete worse. Start logging AXP hours immediately and note ARE divisions in progress. If you spent time on site, highlight it: firms value people who understand how what they draw gets built.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write an architect resume with no experience?

A portfolio showing technical capability — plans, sections, one construction detail — alongside renders. Detail internships by project phase and learn Revit at production level.

Should the resume be highly visual?

It should be impeccable and hierarchical. An illegible CV suggests you cannot organize information, a core architectural competency.

How many projects should the portfolio have?

Four to six, developed. Fewer projects with technical depth beat many at image level.

Should I include unbuilt projects?

Yes, labeled as design or competition. But if you have built work, lead with it — it carries more weight.

Revit or AutoCAD?

Both, but Revit is the standard in mid and large firms. If you only know AutoCAD, that is your learning priority.
