How to write a carpenter resume that proves craft and finish quality

In carpentry the work speaks for itself — but first someone has to see it. The shop foreman or general contractor reading your resume wants to know what type of carpentry you do (rough, finish, cabinetry, framing), which machines you run, and whether you finish on schedule. A resume that names neither specialty nor equipment gets you filed as a helper.

What the foreman or contractor looks for

  • Type of carpentry. Rough framing, finish carpentry, cabinetry, trim and molding, doors and windows, formwork, restoration. Each is a distinct trade.
  • Machines and tools. Table saw, jointer, planer, router, CNC, nail guns, track saw. Name what you operate safely and independently.
  • Materials and finishes. Hardwood, plywood, MDF, melamine. Staining, lacquer, veneer, hardware installation. The finish is what the client sees.
  • Blueprint reading and measurement. Reading construction or shop drawings, field measuring, material takeoffs. A carpenter who measures wrong costs material and time.
  • Portfolio. Photos of finished work. In this trade it is close to mandatory: contractors want to see the finish, not read about it.

Mistakes that get a carpenter resume rejected

  • "Carpenter" with no specialty. A framer and a finish carpenter are not interchangeable. Specify what you do.
  • Not naming machines. It determines level and pay. If you run a jointer and router, say so; if you work mainly hand tools, say that honestly too.
  • Omitting a portfolio. Without photos the contractor cannot judge your finish quality. Three good photos beat a paragraph.
  • Not stating tool ownership. Many jobs expect you to bring your own. If you have a full set, it is a differentiator.
  • Leaving out safety. OSHA 10, PPE, saw safety, dust collection. Formal shops require it and accidents here are severe.

If you are starting in carpentry

This is a trade where visual evidence outweighs paper. Even without formal jobs, photograph what you have built: a piece of furniture, shelving, a repaired door. Three well-lit pieces demonstrate finish level. List trade school or apprenticeship hours if you have them, and if you learned under an experienced carpenter, say so with duration and work type. Name the machines you have operated even in someone else's shop, honestly about your level. Willingness to start as a helper while you learn is the standard entry path.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write a carpenter resume with no formal experience?

With photos of what you have built, even for your own home. Add trade school or apprenticeship time, and be honest about which machines you run and at what level.

Do I need a portfolio?

Practically yes. Carpentry hires on finish quality, and finish is visual. Three well-lit photos are enough to start.

Should I write carpenter or cabinetmaker?

Cabinetmaker implies fine furniture and high-end finish. Use the term that matches your real work and the posting.

Should I list self-employed jobs?

Yes, with work type, materials and whether you installed on site. It shows autonomy, measuring skill and direct client handling.

How long should it be?

One page, with a portfolio link or mention. Specialty and machines at the top, then jobs by type and material.
