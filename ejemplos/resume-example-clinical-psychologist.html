How to write a clinical psychologist resume that shows orientation and training

In clinical psychology, whoever is hiring — a practice, hospital, agency or school — needs three concrete things: your therapeutic orientation, the populations you serve, and the training that supports your interventions. A resume that says "provided psychological services" with no orientation or population allows no evaluation, and in this profession specific training is what separates a clinician from a good listener.

What the clinical director evaluates

  • Degree, license and status. PhD or PsyD, program and year, state license with number, or license-eligible status with supervised hours completed. First section.
  • Theoretical orientation and training. CBT, DBT, ACT, EMDR, psychodynamic, family systems. With the formal training behind it, not just affinity.
  • Populations and presenting concerns. Adults, children and adolescents, couples, families. And concerns: anxiety, depression, trauma, grief, substance use, behavioral disorders.
  • Assessment instruments. Psychometric measures you administer and interpret, structured clinical interviews, psychological report writing.
  • Supervision and ethics. Supervised hours accrued, ethics training, risk assessment and confidentiality management, mandated reporting.

Mistakes that get a psychologist resume rejected

  • Not stating orientation. It is the first question a clinical director asks. Without it, they cannot know how you work.
  • "Psychological services" with no population or concern. Treating adolescents with conduct issues and adults with grief require different training.
  • Listing measures you cannot interpret. Verified in the technical interview and in practice. List only those you administer with training.
  • Omitting supervised hours. In clinical work it signals training rigor, especially in early career.
  • Blending clinical and organizational work. They are distinct branches with distinct competencies. If you did both, separate them clearly.

If you are early career or completing licensure hours

Your practicum and internship are clinical experience: detail them by site, population, presenting concerns and approximate caseload, noting supervision. Specify supervised hours accrued — it is what clinical directors weigh most in a junior profile. Volunteer work on crisis lines, community programs or psychosocial support counts with the same detail. State your licensure status and EPPP progress clearly. Invest in postdoctoral training in a specific modality: in psychology, specialization is what generates real employability. And be careful not to claim competencies you are not yet trained for.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write a psychologist resume early in my career?

Detail practicum and internship by site, population, presenting concerns and caseload, noting supervision. State supervised hours accrued and your licensure status clearly.

Should I state my theoretical orientation?

Yes, it is the first question asked. Back it with formal training: claiming CBT without specific coursework or supervision shows in the interview.

How do I describe cases without breaching confidentiality?

By type and without identifiers: "ongoing treatment of anxiety and depression in young adults". Never include details that could identify anyone.

Should I list every assessment instrument I know?

Only those you administer and interpret with training. Listing measures without real competency is detected immediately.

Should I separate clinical and organizational experience?

Yes, clearly. They are different branches and blending them confuses recruiters about your actual profile.
