How to write a social media manager resume that shows judgment, not just creativity

Anyone can post. What a company pays for is someone who understands the brand, can hold a conversation with an angry customer in public, and knows which content drives business versus which just entertains. Your resume must prove editorial judgment and crisis handling, not just that you know Canva.

What the brand manager looks for

  • Accounts managed and industry. How many brands, which sector, what community size. Running a 2,000-follower B2B account and a 200,000-follower retail account are different jobs.
  • Community results. Growth, engagement rate, reach, website traffic from social, leads or sales attributed. With the time period. If you lack exact figures, describe the trajectory honestly.
  • Crisis and customer care. One publicly handled complaint outweighs a thousand posts. It is the competency that reassures a brand manager most.
  • Content production. Copywriting, basic design (Canva, Figma), short-form video editing, product photography. And at what cadence you produced.
  • Management tools. Meta Business Suite, Hootsuite, Sprout Social, Buffer, Later, plus native analytics on each platform.

Mistakes that get a social media resume rejected

  • "Managed social media" with no detail. It does not say which brands, which sector or with what result. It is the most repeated phrase in the field.
  • Vanity metrics only. Follower counts without context say nothing. Lead with engagement, traffic and conversion where you have them.
  • Not showing the work. Without links to managed accounts or a portfolio, your resume is a claim with no evidence. Include what you can share with permission.
  • Ignoring the customer service side. Much of the job is answering DMs and complaints. Omitting it suggests you only post.
  • Blurring into paid media. If you did not manage ad budget, do not claim you ran paid campaigns. It surfaces in the interview.

If you are seeking your first social media role

This is one of the few fields where you can build experience in weeks. Manage social for a local business, a nonprofit or your own project, and document the trajectory: what you found, what you changed, what happened. A personal account with a real audience counts if the content is relevant to the sector. Learn short-form video editing — it is the most in-demand skill and the one fewest candidates genuinely have. Build a portfolio with three pieces: a content calendar, one campaign, and one example of handling negative comments.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write a social media resume with no experience?

Manage a real account — a local business, nonprofit or your own project — and document before and after. Add a portfolio with a content calendar, one campaign and a comment-handling example.

Should I write social media manager or community manager?

Use the posting's term. US listings mostly say "social media manager"; "community manager" often implies forum or Discord community work. Do not use them interchangeably.

Does my personal account count as experience?

If it has a real audience and content relevant to the sector you are applying to, yes. If it is purely personal, leave it off.

Which metrics should I include?

Engagement rate, reach, growth and — if available — traffic and conversions, with time period and context. Avoid listing follower count alone.

Do I need design and video editing skills?

Yes, at a functional level. Most roles expect you to produce content, not just schedule it. Canva and short-form video editing are today's baseline.
