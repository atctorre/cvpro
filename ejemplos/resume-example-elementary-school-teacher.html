How to write a teacher resume that shows classroom results

Elementary teacher, primary teacher, classroom teacher — the principal hiring reads dozens of resumes with the same title and the same degree. What makes them look up is evidence that your students grew, that you run a classroom without chaos, and that you work well with families and the system. Your resume has to speak in results, not in passion for teaching.

What principals look for on a teacher resume

  • Grades and subjects taught. With years: "3rd and 4th grade, 2021–2025". Each level has its own pedagogy and principals hire for exact fit.
  • Student growth. Gains on benchmark assessments (MAP, DIBELS, state tests), reading-level growth, reduced retention, visible project outcomes. With numbers when they are honest.
  • Classroom management and differentiation. Large groups, IEP and 504 students, English learners, Title I contexts. One strategy that worked is worth more than "patient".
  • Planning and assessment. Standards-aligned lesson plans, rubrics, formative assessment, platforms (Google Classroom, Seesaw, Canvas, district LMS).
  • Families and team. Parent conferences, collaboration with special education and counselors, grade-level teams, school committees. Schools hire people who add to the team.

Mistakes that get a teacher resume rejected

  • The passion-driven summary. "Passionate educator committed to student success" is on every resume. Replace it with a result: "3rd-grade class moved from 40% to 85% proficient in reading comprehension in one year".
  • Not specifying grades or subjects. "Elementary teacher" spans K–5 or K–6. Without detail, the principal cannot tell if you fit the opening.
  • Omitting license and endorsements. State teaching license, grade band, endorsements (ESL, special education, reading). Missing it is an automatic screen-out in public schools.
  • Typos. You will teach children to write. A resume with errors closes the door before the interview.
  • Three pages of workshops. Choose the five most relevant and recent. Principals do not read the full list of PD from 2015.

If you are a newly certified teacher

Your student teaching is teaching experience — present it that way: school, grade, duration, class size, units you planned and assessed yourself, one strategy with a result. Tutoring, after-school programs, summer camps and paraprofessional work all count. Add short certifications principals value: CPR/First Aid, ESL or reading endorsements, trauma-informed practices. State your license status and any additional grade bands or subjects you are certified for. Openness to Title I or hard-to-staff schools opens many more doors at the start.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write a teacher resume with no experience?

Present student teaching as experience: grades, class size, lesson plans and one strategy with a result. Add tutoring, after-school work or paraprofessional roles. Principals look for classroom evidence, not years.

Should I include student test scores?

Yes, when they are honest and you can explain them: "Class average grew 1.5 grade levels on MAP Reading". Never invent numbers; use observable outcomes if you lack data.

Where does my teaching license go?

First section, with state, grade band, endorsements and expiration. It is the first filter in public school hiring.

Is technology or a second language worth listing?

Yes. Teachers who can support ESL, dual-language or edtech integration are among the most sought after. List with your real level.

How long should it be?

One page under five years; two maximum with more experience. Grades, results and credentials on page one.
