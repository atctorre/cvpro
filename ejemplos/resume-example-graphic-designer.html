How to write a graphic designer resume when the portfolio decides

In graphic design, the resume exists to get your portfolio opened. Nobody hires from a CV; they hire from what they see. But a poorly designed resume closes the door before the portfolio, because it is your first assessable design piece. And the most common mistake is not the layout — it is failing to explain what problem each project solved.

What the art director evaluates

  • The portfolio, first and foremost. Visible, working link with 6–10 selected pieces. Fewer and stronger. A twenty-piece portfolio gets judged by its weakest work.
  • A real specialty. Brand identity, editorial, packaging, digital/UI, motion, illustration. Unfocused generalists compete worse than specialists.
  • Tools with honest levels. Illustrator, Photoshop, InDesign, Figma, After Effects. And the real level: producing independently is not the same as assisting.
  • Production knowledge. Print preparation, color profiles, bleed and formats, adapting across media. A design that cannot go to press is not finished.
  • Judgment, not just aesthetics. Explaining why you made a design decision outweighs the piece alone. It separates a designer from someone who operates software.

Mistakes that get a designer resume rejected

  • An overloaded resume. Trying to prove creativity through the CV usually produces an unreadable document. Restraint is a design skill.
  • Portfolio without context. Showing a piece without stating the client or the problem turns your work into decoration.
  • Software skill bars. "Photoshop 90%" means nothing and reads as filler. State years and type of use.
  • Broken link or a heavy PDF portfolio. If it does not open on the art director's phone, it does not get seen. Use a light web link.
  • Unadapted class projects. Academic exercises are recognizable. If they are all you have, present them as cases with a problem and a solution.

If you are a junior or self-taught designer

Your portfolio is everything, but it must be a portfolio of cases, not a gallery. Take three projects — even self-initiated or for a local business — and present them with problem, process and outcome. Rebranding a real local business and explaining each decision beats twenty class exercises. Specialize in something: juniors who claim to do everything compete worse. And treat your own resume as a design piece: it is the first evidence of your judgment and it will be judged as such.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write a graphic designer resume with no experience?

With three well-presented cases — problem, process, outcome — even self-initiated or for a local business. Specialize in one area and treat your resume as a design piece.

Should the resume be highly creative?

It should be impeccable and readable. An overloaded CV suggests you cannot prioritize information, a core design competency.

How many pieces should the portfolio have?

Six to ten, curated. It is judged by the weakest, so remove anything below the level of your best work.

Should I include university projects?

Only if they meet the bar and are framed as cases with a problem and solution. Context-free exercises subtract.

Do I need UI and digital design skills?

Increasingly, yes — many graphic roles now include digital deliverables. If you use Figma, list it; if not, it is the tool to learn first.
