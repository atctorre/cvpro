How to write an HR resume when the reader screens resumes for a living

Your resume will be reviewed by someone who reviews resumes every day. That means any formatting slip, filler phrase or unsupported claim is spotted instantly. An HR resume is itself a work sample: if yours is generic, you have just demonstrated you cannot tell a strong resume from a weak one.

What the HR manager looks for

  • Functional areas you own. Recruiting, payroll, benefits, training and development, employee relations, compensation, HRIS administration. Nobody is strong in all; state where you are.
  • Headcount supported. How many employees and across how many locations. Supporting 40 people and 900 are different jobs.
  • HR metrics. Turnover, absenteeism, time-to-fill, cost-per-hire, training hours, retention. If you worked with them and moved them, it is your strongest argument.
  • Compliance knowledge. FLSA, FMLA, EEO, I-9 verification, ADA accommodations, state wage law. An analyst who knows compliance prevents expensive claims.
  • Systems. Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, ADP, BambooHR, Greenhouse or other ATS, advanced Excel. Name them.

Mistakes that get an HR resume rejected

  • A generic resume. The gravest error in this field, because it demonstrates you do not know your own craft. Your resume is your portfolio.
  • "Assisted with HR processes". It says neither which function nor at what autonomy. Specify complete processes you owned.
  • No metrics. HR has professionalized: it is measured now. Without turnover, time-to-fill or absenteeism, you read as administrative, not analytical.
  • Omitting compliance experience. The employment-law side is where company risk lives. Handling terminations or investigations is a weighty credential.
  • Typos. You will write internal communications and formal letters. Immediately disqualifying.

If you are seeking your first HR role

Entry-level HR is crowded, so specificity is the differentiator. If you interned, detail the function and volume: interviews conducted, files administered, complete processes you ran. If you worked in administration, customer service or team coordination, there are real transferable competencies: handling confidential information, documentation, dealing with people in difficult moments. Add employment-law coursework or an entry HR certification (aPHR, SHRM-CP eligibility) and Excel with a certificate. Avoid "I enjoy working with people" — everyone writes it.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write an HR resume with no experience?

Detail internships with function and concrete volume. Highlight genuine transferable skills — confidentiality, documentation, difficult conversations — and add employment-law training plus Excel.

Should I write HR analyst, generalist or coordinator?

Use the posting's term. "Generalist" implies multiple functions; "analyst" implies data and metrics. Choose what reflects your actual work.

What metrics do I use if we did not track them?

The volumes you do know: requisitions filled, files administered, employees trained, headcount supported. Never invent a turnover percentage.

Should I mention handling terminations?

Yes, in professional language: "supported separations in accordance with company policy and applicable law". It shows you handle the sensitive side.

Is an aPHR or SHRM certification worth it?

For entry roles, aPHR signals commitment and often passes an initial filter. SHRM-CP or PHR carry more weight once you have experience.
