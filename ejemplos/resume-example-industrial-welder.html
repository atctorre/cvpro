How to write a welder resume that proves processes and weld quality

In welding, your pay is not set by years — it is set by the processes you run, the positions you weld in, and whether your welds pass inspection. A pipe welder certified in 6G earns far more than someone who only welds flat. Your resume has to state exactly which processes, which positions and which materials, because that is what the shop foreman reads first.

What the shop foreman or superintendent looks for

  • Processes you run. SMAW (stick), GMAW/MIG, GTAW/TIG, FCAW (flux-core). State which you run independently and on what materials. It is the number-one data point.
  • Welding positions. Flat, horizontal, vertical, overhead, 5G, 6G for pipe. Position defines technical level and pay.
  • Materials and thickness. Carbon steel, stainless, aluminum, exotic alloys, gauge ranges. Aluminum and stainless demand different technique and pay better.
  • Certifications. AWS D1.1 structural, ASME Section IX, API 1104 for pipeline, with test dates. Most have continuity requirements — list dates.
  • Quality record. Visual inspection, RT/UT results, reject or repair rate. Welds that pass X-ray without rework is your strongest argument.

Mistakes that get a welder resume rejected

  • Not specifying processes. "Welder" without stick, MIG or TIG gives no way to place you. It is the first filter.
  • Omitting positions. Without them the foreman assumes flat only — the most basic level.
  • Leaving off expired certifications. If they lapsed, list them with dates and note you can requalify. Omitting suggests you never held them.
  • Not mentioning blueprint and symbol reading. Interpreting a weld symbol is real technical competence. Many welders assume it goes without saying.
  • Ignoring safety. Hot work permits, confined space, fall protection, PPE. It is high-risk work and formal employers require documented training.

If you just completed welding school

Certification outweighs years in this trade. List your program with school, hours and processes learned. What will move your pay fastest is getting qualified: a welder qualification test in a specific process and position is a document that changes your rate immediately. If you have welded on informal jobs, describe it with process, material and part type. Mention whether you own a hood and hand tools, and whether you can work at heights or in confined spaces — both open better-paying positions.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write a welder resume with no experience?

Detail your training with processes and practice hours, and get qualified as soon as possible: a certification in a specific process and position outweighs years without one.

Which process should I master first?

Depends on the sector: stick for structural and field work, MIG for shop production, TIG for stainless and precision aluminum. TIG generally pays more but demands more skill.

Should I list expired certifications?

Yes, with dates and a note that you can requalify. Omitting them suggests you never held them, which is worse.

How do I show quality without inspection reports?

Through work type and its requirements: "pressure pipe welding with visual and radiographic inspection, no rejects". If you lack data, describe the part type and the code you worked to.

Is cutting experience worth mentioning?

Yes. Many roles combine welding with plasma or oxy-fuel cutting and fit-up. Broadening scope opens more openings.
