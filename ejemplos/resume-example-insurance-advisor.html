How to write an insurance agent resume that proves production and retention

Insurance is consultative selling of a product nobody wants until they need it. That is why hiring managers measure two things: how much you produce and how much of your book renews. Persistency — the percentage of policies still in force after a year — says more about an agent than first-year volume, because selling once is easy and retaining is not.

What the agency manager evaluates

  • Production. Policies written, premium volume, quota attainment. It is the entry metric.
  • Persistency and retention. Percentage of book that renews. It separates a professional agent from a first-year seller.
  • Lines you write. Life, health, property and casualty, auto, commercial, disability, annuities. Each line has different technical logic and sales cycle.
  • Licensure. State insurance licenses by line (Life, Health, P&C), with states and status. Series licenses if applicable. Legal requirement to operate.
  • Prospecting and book ownership. How you generate clients: referrals, personal network, cold prospecting, partnerships. An agent with an existing book is valuable from day one.

Mistakes that get an insurance resume rejected

  • A resume with no numbers. It is a numbers profession. Without production or persistency, you read as a beginner regardless of years.
  • Not specifying lines. Selling auto and selling commercial life are different trades with opposite sales cycles.
  • Omitting license details. Without state licensure you cannot legally operate. Its absence halts the process.
  • Talking only about sales, not service. The agent who walks a client through a claim keeps that client. Mention claims support if you did it.
  • Hiding weak years. Insurance has cycles. Show the trend; unexplained gaps are what disqualify.

If you are entering insurance

Insurance is among the sectors that hires most readily without experience, because agencies train their own. What they screen for is commercial capability and a network. If you come from sales, customer service or any direct-contact role, those competencies transfer — name them as such. Get your state license as soon as possible; many carriers sponsor the process and pay for the exam. Mention your network honestly, because the first year runs on it. And choose a line to specialize in: agents who try to sell everything usually sell little.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write an insurance agent resume with no experience?

Highlight transferable commercial skills from sales or service, mention your network, and prioritize obtaining state licensure — the legal requirement to sell.

Should I write insurance agent or advisor?

Use the posting's term. "Agent" often implies representing one carrier; "broker" or "advisor" implies working across carriers.

What numbers do I use if production was modest?

The trend and persistency: "book retention above agency average". Retaining well compensates for producing less.

Should I mention claims support?

Yes. It is where the client decides whether to renew. An agent who supports the claim retains, and managers know it.

Is licensing required?

Yes. Every state requires licensure by line to sell insurance. List each state and line with status.
