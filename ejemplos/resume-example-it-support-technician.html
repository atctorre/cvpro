How to write an IT support resume that shows real resolution

IT support, help desk, desktop support, service desk — it is the entry point into IT and one of the highest-applicant roles. The IT manager hiring wants someone who resolves tickets without escalating everything, documents what they fix, and stays patient with a frustrated user. Your resume has to show volume, specific technologies, and the ability to explain technical things in plain language.

What the IT manager looks for on a support resume

  • Ticket volume and tier. Tickets per day or week, first-contact resolution rate, average resolution time. Tier 1 and Tier 2 mean different things; specify which.
  • Specific technologies. Windows 10/11, macOS, Active Directory, Microsoft 365, Intune, Google Workspace, basic networking, printers, VPN, endpoint security, ticketing (ServiceNow, Jira Service Management, Freshservice). Name them.
  • Hardware and field work. Imaging and deployment, component replacement, cabling, new-hire setup, remote support tools (TeamViewer, AnyDesk, RDP, Quick Assist).
  • Documentation. Knowledge base articles, SOPs, user guides. A technician who documents reduces future tickets — managers know this.
  • Certifications. CompTIA A+, Network+, ITIL 4 Foundation, Microsoft MD-102, Google IT Support. Even one changes how the resume is read.

Mistakes that get an IT support resume rejected

  • "Supported end users" with no technologies. The filter searches for names: Active Directory, Microsoft 365, ServiceNow. If they are absent, you do not pass even if you know them.
  • No numbers. "Handled incidents" says nothing. "40 tickets daily with 80% first-contact resolution" does.
  • Listing technologies you cannot defend. The technical interview asks about each one. List only what you can discuss.
  • Omitting the customer service side. Support is half technical, half people. One example of a difficult user handled well carries weight.
  • Cluttered design. IT managers value clarity. Clean resume, one page, clear sections.

If you are looking for your first IT job

Help desk is the classic entry point and hires without experience if you prove two things: you can troubleshoot and you can work with people. For the first, a certification (CompTIA A+ or Google IT Support are the most recognized starting points) and a home lab you can describe: a configured home network, a virtual machine setup, devices you repaired for others. For the second, any customer-facing job. If you fixed computers for family and friends, write it as "independent IT support" with the problem types you resolved and approximate user count.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write an IT support resume with no experience?

Earn an entry certification (CompTIA A+ or Google IT Support), describe your home lab or informal repairs as real experience, and highlight customer-facing work. Managers hire for troubleshooting ability and communication.

Should I write IT support, help desk or service desk?

Use the posting's term. In your summary: "Tier 1 IT support (help desk / service desk) technician" to cover the variants.

Does informal experience count?

Yes, if described precisely: "independent support for 15 home users: OS reinstalls, malware removal, home network setup". It is real experience.

Which certification should I get first?

CompTIA A+ is the most widely recognized for support roles; Google IT Support is more accessible and also respected. ITIL 4 Foundation adds value for larger enterprise environments.

How long should the resume be?

One page. Certifications and technologies near the top, then experience with ticket volume and resolution numbers.
