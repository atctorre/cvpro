How to write a physical therapist resume that shows clinical reasoning

A physical therapist is not hired for a list of techniques but for the ability to evaluate, reason and adapt a plan of care when the patient is not progressing. The clinic director looks for clinical reasoning, practice settings and evidence of patient outcomes. A resume full of modalities with no populations or outcomes reads like a new graduate's, regardless of years.

What the clinic director evaluates

  • License and credentials. State PT license with number and expiration, DPT or MPT degree, board certifications (OCS, SCS, NCS, GCS), CEUs current. First section.
  • Settings and populations. Outpatient orthopedic, inpatient rehab, skilled nursing, home health, pediatrics, sports, neuro. Each is a distinct profile; specify which you have worked.
  • Evaluation and clinical reasoning. Outcome measures used (FOTO, LEFS, DASH, Berg), functional goals, discharge criteria. It distinguishes a therapist from a modality technician.
  • Productivity and documentation. Visits per day, documentation compliance, EMR systems (WebPT, Raintree, Epic). Clinics run on both throughput and clean notes.
  • Patient outcomes. Functional recovery, adherence, return to sport or work. Concrete examples without identifiable information.

Mistakes that get a PT resume rejected

  • Modality lists with no context. Twenty techniques with no population or training behind them reduces credibility. Fewer with certification convinces more.
  • Omitting license details. It is a legal requirement to practice. Its absence is the first red flag.
  • Not specifying setting. "Physical therapist" spans pediatric neuro to sports outpatient. Without detail, the director cannot tell if you fit.
  • Confusing modalities with treatment. Applying ultrasound is not a plan of care. Write about evaluation, goals and progression.
  • Leading with weekend courses. Continuing education without clinical caseload does not replace treated patients. Select the relevant ones.

If you are a new graduate physical therapist

Your clinical rotations are experience: detail them by rotation with facility, setting, patient population and caseload. Include any volunteer work with sports teams, nursing homes or community clinics with the same detail. Add the certifications clinics value in new grads: BLS, dry needling where permitted, specific exercise or taping courses. Be honest about technique proficiency — mastering three beats listing fifteen. State your license status and NPTE date clearly, and note openness to varied schedules; most outpatient clinics run extended hours.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write a physical therapist resume with no experience?

Detail clinical rotations by setting, population and caseload. Add volunteer or sports coverage and relevant certifications. Be precise about proficiency with each technique.

How many techniques should I list?

Only those you can perform with training behind them and defend in a practical. Three well-supported beat fifteen listed.

Should I list weekend continuing education?

Only what is relevant to the role, with provider and hours. A long list of short courses without clinical caseload reduces credibility.

How do I show outcomes without patient data?

Describe case type and functional result without identifying anyone: "return to sport following ACL reconstruction within expected timeline".

Is home health experience worth mentioning?

Yes. Home health demands autonomy and clinical judgment, and many organizations hire for it. State caseload and patient type.
