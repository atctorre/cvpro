How to write a server resume that gets you the floor trial

Restaurants hire fast and fire fast. The manager reading your resume wants three things in ten seconds: can you handle a full section on a Saturday night, do you sell (apps, drinks, desserts), and will you actually show up. After that comes the floor trial — and the paper stops mattering.

What restaurant managers look for on a server resume

  • Restaurant type and volume. Quick service, casual, fine dining, hotel, banquet. Section size, covers per shift. Fine dining pace and buffet pace have nothing in common.
  • Suggestive selling. If you raised check averages recommending appetizers, wine or desserts, that is sales. Say it with what you can measure: "check average above section mean".
  • POS and payment handling. Toast, Aloha, Square, Micros — name it. Tip handling, split checks, cash-out at close.
  • Complaint recovery. A cold plate, a long wait, an upset table. One example resolved without calling the manager beats "excellent customer service".
  • Food safety and alcohol certifications. Food handler card, ServSafe, TIPS or state alcohol certification. They save the employer paperwork and time.

Mistakes that get a server resume rejected

  • Not stating restaurant type or size. "Server at restaurant" places you nowhere. "Server at 40-table casual dining, 120+ covers per shift" does.
  • Leaving out sales. Many servers do not see themselves as salespeople. Managers do. If you upsold, write it.
  • Hiding job hopping. Normal in this industry. Use real dates and show what you learned at each place.
  • No availability. Nights, weekends, holidays, closing shifts. If it is not there, the manager assumes you cannot.
  • Two pages. One. The manager reads it standing at the host stand.

If you have never worked as a server

It is one of the most common entry-level hires, but the manager needs to see you handle pressure and treat guests well. Any customer-facing job counts: cashier, host, retail, food prep at a family business, event staff. Get your food handler card before applying — it is cheap, fast and puts you ahead. Above all, make night and weekend availability explicit: it is the number-one filter.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write a server resume with no experience?

Lead with night and weekend availability, any customer-facing work, and a food handler card. That gets you on the list for a floor trial.

Should I write server, waiter or waitress?

Use "server" — it is the standard industry term and what most postings use. Filters match the posting's wording.

Is Spanish or another language worth listing?

Yes. In many US restaurants a second language helps with both guests and kitchen communication. List it with your real level.

Should I list a job I held for two months?

Yes if you learned something (a POS system, wine service, high volume). If you left in the first week, leaving it off is fine.

How do I show I sell?

With one concrete line: "Consistently recommended appetizers and specials; check average above section mean". Do not invent percentages you cannot back up.
