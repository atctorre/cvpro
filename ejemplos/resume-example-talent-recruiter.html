How to write a recruiter resume that proves hiring results

Recruiting is a sales function wearing an HR badge: it is measured in requisitions closed, time-to-fill and quality of hire. Whoever is hiring you wants to know how many roles you closed, of what type, and how fast. If your resume describes the selection process in the abstract instead of your numbers, it files you as a coordinator.

What the talent leader looks for

  • Requisition volume and type. How many you closed monthly or annually, and at what level: high-volume hourly, professional, technical, executive. Hiring 40 warehouse associates and one CFO demand opposite skills.
  • Time-to-fill. Average days from requisition to accepted offer. It is the headline metric in recruiting.
  • Quality of hire. 90-day and 6-month retention, hiring manager satisfaction. Hiring fast and badly is worse than hiring slowly.
  • Sourcing channels. LinkedIn Recruiter, Boolean search, job boards, referrals, university relations, direct outreach. Active sourcing outweighs posting and waiting.
  • Full-cycle ownership. Intake meetings with hiring managers, screening, behavioral interviews, assessments, references, offer and negotiation.

Mistakes that get a recruiter resume rejected

  • Describing the process instead of results. "Posted jobs, screened resumes, scheduled interviews" is the job description. Your numbers are what differentiate you.
  • No time-to-fill. It is the metric every talent leader asks about. If you lack the exact figure, give an honest range.
  • Not specifying candidate level. High-volume hiring and executive search are different trades with different metrics.
  • Omitting offer negotiation. Closing a candidate who held a competing offer is a real commercial skill. Many leave it out.
  • A resume with errors. You are the person who judges resumes. Yours is your most direct credential.

If you want to break into recruiting

Recruiting values two things you can demonstrate without formal experience: structured conversation skill and commercial persistence. If you come from sales, customer service or hospitality, those competencies transfer directly — name them that way. If you interned in HR, detail how many resumes you screened and interviews you conducted. Learn Boolean search on LinkedIn: it is a concrete skill, it shows in the interview, and few junior candidates have it. Add training in behavioral interviewing, the sector's standard methodology.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write a recruiter resume with no experience?

Highlight transferable skills from sales or service, detail internships with resume and interview volume, and learn Boolean sourcing on LinkedIn — a concrete, differentiating skill.

Should I write recruiter or talent acquisition specialist?

Use the posting's term. "Talent acquisition" often implies active sourcing and employer branding; "recruiter" is the more searched, general term.

What if I do not have exact time-to-fill?

Give an honest range: "average fill time of 15–25 days depending on role level". Better than omitting or inventing.

How do I show quality of hire?

Through retention: "27 of 30 hires passed their 90-day review". If you lack the data, cite hiring manager feedback.

Is high-volume recruiting worth highlighting?

Yes, if genuine — it shows you can manage simultaneous pipelines. But do not blend it with executive search, which is a different competency.
