How to write a travel agent resume that proves sales and systems

The agency hiring you knows the client could book online alone. That is why they no longer pay for ticket issuance: they pay for someone who sells complex itineraries, fixes a flight change at eleven at night, and makes that client come back. Your resume has to prove sales ability and booking-system command, not enthusiasm for travel.

What the agency manager looks for

  • GDS proficiency. Sabre, Amadeus, Apollo/Galileo, Travelport. It is the number-one technical filter and determines whether they must train you from zero. State level and years.
  • Product specialization. Air, packages, cruises, corporate travel, groups, luxury, destination weddings. Each carries different margin and complexity.
  • Sales results. Booking volume, revenue, quota attainment, average booking value, repeat clients. It is a commercial profession and gets measured as one.
  • Disruption management. Cancellations, rebooking, schedule changes, airline claims. One resolved example beats any adjective.
  • Destinations and suppliers. Regions you know, consolidators and tour operators you worked with, negotiated rates. Product knowledge accelerates the sale.

Mistakes that get a travel agent resume rejected

  • "Passionate about travel" as a summary. The most repeated phrase in the sector and it says nothing about the job, which is selling and solving problems.
  • Not naming the GDS. If you know Sabre or Amadeus and do not write it, the filter treats it as nonexistent. It is the most searched term.
  • Omitting sales numbers. Without volume or quota attainment you read as counter staff, not a commercial advisor.
  • Not specifying client type. Corporate and leisure are opposite sales: one wants policy compliance and efficiency, the other experience and price.
  • Leaving out after-sales service. Clients return because of how you handled the problem, not because of the perfect booking.

If you are seeking your first agency role

GDS training is your best investment: a Sabre or Amadeus course changes how your resume reads because it is the filter agencies apply first. Add any hospitality or tourism education, and rewrite sales or customer service experience in industry language: quoting, follow-up, closing, after-sales. If you have traveled, do not list it as a hobby — convert it into concrete destination knowledge: routes, seasons, entry requirements. If you speak a second language, lead with it; in corporate and international leisure travel it is often decisive.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write a travel agent resume with no experience?

Take a Sabre or Amadeus course and put it in the first section — it is the technical filter agencies apply. Rewrite sales experience in industry language and highlight language skills.

Should I write travel agent or travel advisor?

Use the posting's term. "Advisor" often implies consultative luxury or custom travel; "agent" is the more searched, general term.

Is GDS knowledge essential?

For traditional and corporate agencies, effectively yes. For online or package-focused agencies it may not be, but knowing it widens your options considerably.

How do I show I sell?

With volume or quota: "consistently met monthly booking targets through peak season". If you lack figures, describe product type and client volume.

Does personal travel count?

Only if converted into product knowledge: destinations, seasons, entry requirements, property types. As a hobby, it adds nothing.
