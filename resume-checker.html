How the check works

  1. Upload or paste. PDF, Word (.docx) or plain text, up to 5 MB. The text is extracted in your browser before anything is sent anywhere.
  2. The extracted text is scored. A model reads that flattened text against a fixed set of criteria — generic clichés, first-person phrasing, missing numbers and results, missing keywords for your field, and confusing structure — and returns a score from 0 to 100. This is not a login to an employer ATS.
  3. Optional: paste a job description. You get a second score for overlap with that specific posting. It measures how your resume matches that job, not a universal ATS pass.

What the score measures

A model scores the extracted text against a fixed rubric; it does not connect to Greenhouse, Workday or Taleo. Before that happens, the text you uploaded is flattened into a single run of plain text — line breaks and spacing are collapsed, so the model never sees columns, tables or where something sat on the page. It can only judge what made it into that flattened text, not your layout.

What it checksWhat fails the check
Generic clichésStock adjectives with nothing backing them — "responsible," "proactive," "committed" — instead of what you actually did.
First-person phrasing"I managed...", "I was responsible for..." instead of direct, resume-style phrasing.
Missing numbers and resultsDuties listed with no outcome attached — no amount, no percentage, no count, no timeframe.
Job keywordsLittle or no overlap with the terms used in postings for your field, or with the job description you pasted.
Confusing structureSections, dates or responsibilities that are hard to follow even once the text is flattened and in front of the model.

This is a different question from whether the file parses at all — a two-column layout or a scanned image can keep your text from being extracted in the first place, before any of the above ever gets evaluated. That failure mode is covered next.

Formatting mistakes that break parsing

  • Two-column layout. Many systems read left to right across the full width of the page, not down one column at a time, so text from both columns gets merged out of order.
  • Tables used as layout. A table used to line up job titles, dates and bullets gets extracted cell by cell, often in a different order than a person would read it.
  • Contact info in header/footer. A running header or footer repeats identically on every page, and several systems skip that region — so your phone and email never make it into the extracted text.
  • Text inside images or icons. If your name, a skill bar or a contact icon is really a picture, there is no text in it to extract. When fewer than 120 characters come out of a file, the check stops there and shows an error instead of a score.
  • Non-standard section names. A label like "My Journey" instead of "Work Experience" can stop the system from recognizing the section at all, so everything under it may go uncategorized.
  • Dates as "Present" without a month, or mixed formats. "2021 – Present" has no month to calculate your tenure from, and mixing "2023–2025," "March 2023" and "03/23" in the same resume breaks a date parser built around one format.
  • Unusual fonts / symbol bullets. Decorative fonts and bullet glyphs (triangles, stars, custom icons) can turn into broken or missing characters once the text is extracted, so a clean bullet list comes out unreadable.

Issues found in this example

  • Two-column layout — the name and the skills list sat in side-by-side blocks; a parser reading left to right across the page would merge them before ever reaching "Cashier."
  • Contact info in header/footer — the email and phone lived in a running header, the part of the page several parsers strip because it repeats on every page.
  • Non-standard section names — "My Journey" does not match a label the system is built to recognize, so the work history under it risked being read as unstructured text.
  • Unusual fonts / symbol bullets — the decorative ✦ glyph in front of each duty line can come out as a broken or missing character once the text is extracted, instead of a recognizable bullet.

The two-column block was merged into one column, the header became part of the body text, "My Journey" was renamed "Work Experience" — the section label the system looks for — and the decorative ✦ symbols were replaced with plain hyphens. The job titles and employers were kept exactly as given, and the dates keep the same months and years in a consistent written format (03/2023 → Mar 2023): no new month, payment method or other detail was added that was not already in the original. Only these four formatting issues were fixed. Turning duties into measurable results is a separate, honest step, not something to pad in afterward.

What no tool can promise

This is worth stating plainly: no tool can guarantee a resume will pass every ATS. Each employer configures theirs with its own criteria, and some do not use one at all. What can be done is removing the known technical obstacles, using your industry's vocabulary, and presenting your experience so a human wants to read it. Any service promising a guaranteed pass rate is selling something it cannot deliver.

Frequently asked questions

Is this an ATS login?

No. This is a scoring tool: a model reads your extracted text against a fixed rubric. It does not log in to Greenhouse, Workday, Taleo or any other employer system, and it never sees an employer's account.

Do you store my resume?

No, the text of your resume is not stored. If you ask to have the report emailed to you, we register your email address as a lead at that point. Write to hola@getcvpro.com to request deletion of anything we hold.

What is a good score here?

The scale is 0 to 100. A high score means the extracted text reads like a strong resume: concrete numbers and results, action-oriented phrasing, your field's keywords, and no generic clichés or first-person lines; a low score usually points to one of those content gaps, not to a lack of experience and not to how the file is formatted — the formatting mistakes above affect whether the text gets extracted at all, not this score. It is this tool's own scale — not Jobscan's match rate or any other service's number.

PDF or Word, which parses cleaner?

They use different extractors: PDF text is pulled with pdf.js, reading up to the first 8 pages, while a .docx file is read with mammoth, which has no page limit of its own. Either way, the extracted text is then cut to the same 6,000 characters before it is scored, so a .docx resume does not get a longer analysis than a PDF — it only skips pdf.js's 8-page cutoff during extraction. What actually hurts either format is the same: a scanned image, text placed inside images, or a two-column layout. A native, text-based PDF or .docx file works equally well.

How many checks can I run?

3 free checks a day, with no card required.

Do I need an account?

No. No sign-up and no card to see your score and the issues found.

What does the paid rewrite do differently?

It sends your uploaded text into CVPro's guided builder in "optimize" mode. A model rewrites it in professional language, and a separate, deterministic verifier then checks every line against what you actually provided — it does not add employers, titles or achievements you did not state.

Does the score guarantee an interview?

No. A higher score here means your resume's content is stronger — clear results, no clichés, the right keywords — not that any system finds it more readable. Whether you get an interview still depends on your experience, the job market and the person who reads it. No tool can guarantee a pass through every employer's ATS or a callback.

How do I delete my data?

Write to hola@getcvpro.com. The resume text itself is not stored; if you had requested the report by email, that is the email address we delete on request.
