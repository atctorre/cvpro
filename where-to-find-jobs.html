Where to find jobs: the 6 real channels and which one fits your situation

Almost every piece of job search advice ends the same way: "sign up on the job boards and apply". That is exactly why so many people send a hundred applications and hear nothing — they are using the most saturated channel as if it were the only one.

Job boards are one of six channels, and they are not the best fit for everyone. This guide compares all six honestly — including the part job boards will never write themselves — and tells you which to prioritize based on where you are.

The six channels, with their real advantages and problems

1. Job boards

Indeed, LinkedIn, ZipRecruiter, Glassdoor and the aggregators. This is where the most openings are posted and where everyone goes.

That last part is the problem. A well-paid posting on a major board can draw hundreds of applications within forty-eight hours, and many are filtered automatically before a human opens a file.

They work, but not as your only channel. And they work far better when you apply in the first days and tailor the resume to each posting instead of sending the same one.

✅ Use them with alerts on so you apply early, rather than browsing once a week.

2. Public workforce services

The most underused channel of all. State and local workforce agencies run job centers, hiring events and training programs, often with same-day interviews and a focus on people re-entering the workforce.

The competitive advantage is obvious: far fewer people use this channel than the boards, and employers who post there genuinely need to fill the role.

✅ Find your local workforce center or one-stop career center and ask about their hiring event calendar.

3. Applying directly to the company

Almost nobody does it, which is exactly why it works. Many employers post openings on their own careers page before the boards, or never post them externally at all.

Build a list of twenty or thirty companies you would want to work for, check their careers page weekly, and write to their recruiting team even when nothing is posted.

Competition in this channel is a fraction of what you face on a board.

✅ Start with ten companies in your area and check their careers pages every week.

4. Referrals and contacts

The channel with the highest success rate and the hardest to activate, because it requires asking. A resume that arrives referred by someone inside skips the entire queue.

You do not need a large professional network: former classmates, former coworkers, neighbors, friends of friends. The question is not "do you have a job for me?" but "do you know if your company is hiring for something like this?", which is far easier to answer.

✅ Write to ten specific people with a specific question, not one generic message to everyone.

5. Staffing agencies and recruiters

For temporary, contract and many operational roles, agencies fill positions that never appear publicly. They get paid when they place you, so their incentive is aligned with yours.

The trade-off is that you have less control over which employer you end up with, and temp-to-hire arrangements vary widely in quality.

✅ Register with two or three agencies specialized in your field, not ten generalist ones.

6. Hiring events and job fairs

Run by workforce agencies, community colleges, chambers of commerce and large employers. The advantage is that you speak to a person instead of competing against an automated filter.

For operational and customer-facing roles, this is among the fastest channels: many events interview on the spot.

✅ Bring several printed copies of your resume and arrive early — lines grow quickly after the first hour.

Which channel fits your situation

Not every channel works equally well. This is the combination that makes most sense depending on where you are:

If this is your first job

Prioritize hiring events and workforce centers, because you speak to people and your lack of experience is offset by attitude and availability — things an automated filter cannot see.

Job boards are your second channel, not your first.

✅ Hiring events + workforce center + local networks.

If you need work urgently

Hiring events and staffing agencies, because they have the shortest cycle. At an event you can interview the same day; on a large board the process can take weeks.

Apply through boards too, but do not wait on that response.

✅ Hiring events + staffing agencies + direct application locally.

If you are changing industries

Referrals and direct application. On a board, an automated filter screens you out for lacking the industry history; a person who knows you can explain why you fit.

Here your network is worth more than any job board.

✅ Referrals + direct application + LinkedIn actively worked, not just registered.

If you are employed and looking to move up

LinkedIn and direct application, without rushing. You are not under pressure, so you can target specific companies and wait for the right opening instead of applying to everything.

This is the one case where passive searching works well.

✅ Updated LinkedIn + target company list + referrals.

How to spot a fake job posting

Employment scams have grown sharply, especially on social platforms and messaging apps. These are the signals that should stop you:

They ask for money upfront

For the uniform, the training, the background check, to "hold your spot", or for processing fees. A legitimate employer does not charge you to hire you.

This is the clearest signal and the most common scam.

⚠️ If money is requested at any point in the process, it is a scam. No exceptions.

The pay does not match the role

A posting promising well above market rate for a job with no requirements is usually bait, and sometimes fronts illegal activity.

Compare against other postings for the same role in your area before getting excited.

⚠️ Be suspicious of anything that sounds too good for the work described.

They request sensitive data before hiring you

Bank account numbers, full copies of ID documents, Social Security number or card details during the application stage. None of that is needed for an interview.

Banking and tax details come after a signed offer, through official company channels.

⚠️ Never send bank details or full identity documents through messaging apps.

The whole process happens over messaging, in a hurry

Immediate hiring over WhatsApp or Telegram, no real interview, no verifiable physical address, a company whose name appears nowhere.

The artificial urgency exists so you do not have time to verify anything.

⚠️ Search the company name before replying. If there is no trace, there is no company.

The mistake that makes every channel fail

If you are already applying through several channels and still hear nothing, here are the nine real reasons you are not getting callbacks.

You can work all six channels and still hear nothing if the resume is not ready. On job boards because the automated filter cannot read it; at a hiring event because the recruiter has thirty seconds per person; through a referral because your contact forwards it and it lands looking like everyone else's.

Before investing weeks searching everywhere, spend an hour making sure the document you are sending is in shape. It is what changes the result across every channel at once.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best job board to use?

It depends on your field. Indeed carries the highest volume across most industries, LinkedIn works better for professional and office roles, and industry-specific boards often have less competition. The key is not relying on one: openings are not posted everywhere.

Where do I find jobs with no experience?

Hiring events and workforce centers are where lack of experience matters least, because you speak to a person who can weigh your attitude and availability. Job boards filter on keywords, and there you start at a disadvantage.

Is applying directly on a company website worth it?

Yes, and it is one of the least used channels. Many employers post on their own careers page before the boards. Checking twenty local companies weekly produces better results than applying to two hundred generic postings.

How do I know if a job posting is fake?

The definitive signal is being asked for money for anything: uniform, training, background check or processing. Also be wary if the pay is far above market for the role, if bank details are requested before an offer, or if the entire process happens over messaging with urgency and no real interview.

How long does it take to find a job?

It varies so much by industry, region and seniority that any specific number would be invented. What does shorten the process is working several channels at once instead of relying on boards alone, and applying within the first days of each posting.

Is it better to search online or in person?

Both, for different things. Online gives volume of openings; in person gives access to a decision-maker. For operational and customer-facing roles, in person is usually faster.
